Why Managers Now Need Cybersecurity Knowledge

Three forces are making cybersecurity knowledge mandatory for Indian business managers in 2026: (1) India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act, 2023) — in force from 2025, placing direct legal accountability on organisations handling personal data; (2) SEBI and RBI regulatory pressure on boards and CXOs for cyber governance; (3) rising cyber incident costs — the average cost of a data breach in India crossed ₹17 crore in 2024.

DPDP Act 2023 — Why This Is Urgent

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP) came into force in 2025. It places direct compliance obligations on 'data fiduciaries' — any organisation that handles personal data of Indian residents. Senior managers and CXOs who are not familiar with DPDP compliance requirements are now exposed to regulatory risk. IIM Indore AI+Cyber specifically covers DPDP implications for organisations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What cybersecurity course is best for managers without IT background?

IIM Indore Executive Programme in AI & Cybersecurity for Organizations (~₹1.95L, 6 months) is designed specifically for business leaders and managers without technical IT backgrounds. It teaches cybersecurity governance, risk management, and board-level security strategy — not technical security implementation.

Do managers need to know cybersecurity in 2026?

Yes — cybersecurity is now a board and senior management responsibility, not just IT's concern. SEBI, RBI and India's DPDP Act (2023) place direct accountability on organisations for data security. Senior managers, VPs, and CXOs are increasingly expected to understand cyber risk, participate in risk committees, and make informed security investment decisions.

What does a manager need to know about cybersecurity?

Managers don't need technical hacking skills — they need: understanding of the threat landscape relevant to their business, how to evaluate cyber risk and ROI of security investments, regulatory compliance requirements (DPDP Act, RBI, SEBI), how to communicate security risks to the board, and how to govern vendor security arrangements.
